# Lag during play — findings of 2026-10-08

The user's report: "often laggy while a song is playing". Measured on the
M1 Pro (120 Hz ProMotion panel) with the release binary of 02:07, five
autopilot runs of "[BG-01] Never Gonna Give You Up" (Hard), vsync on,
`BEATBYTE_FPS=1`, muted. No code was changed in this round. The raw logs
and the `sample` traces were not kept.

**Status: open.** Items 1 and 2 below are queued, to be taken up once the
parallel work on PERFORMANCE MODE and the librarian (uncommitted on
2026-10-08) has landed — they touch neighbouring files.

## What the frame costs

On a quiet machine the song holds 120 fps: median 8.3 ms, 99th
percentile ~10 ms. A `sample` of the process shows the main thread and
the compute pool mostly waiting; what work there is spreads thin (mesh
slab allocation and bind groups for meshes that change every frame,
visibility queries). The microphone tempo tracker (`beatbyte-listen`,
for the PA monitors) runs on its own thread at ~2 % of its samples.

## Frame rates come in steps

The panel picks its own refresh rate, and a frame that just misses one
refresh waits for the next: the reported medians are exactly 8.33,
10.0 or 16.0 ms (120, 100, 60 Hz), never in between. "60–100 fps" is the
display in a lower gear, not the game computing slower. In the last run
the panel sat at 100 Hz from the start, on mains power, with no thermal
or power warning recorded (`pmset -g therm`) and Low Power Mode off —
the reason was not found.

## The heavy stalls came from outside the game

| Condition | median | 99th pct | longest |
|---|---|---|---|
| quiet machine | 8.3 ms | ~10 ms | ~25 ms |
| a parallel `cargo test` (run 1) | 15 ms, for 15 s and for 60 s | 50–77 ms | 80–106 ms |
| ten `yes` processes (all cores busy) | 8.3–8.5 ms | ~17 ms | ~30 ms |
| 7 GB of RAM held and touched | 10 ms (panel at 100 Hz) | up to 28 ms | ~51 ms, one 10 s dip to 62 fps |

The machine was 2.9 GB into swap with five or six Claude sessions and
`rustc` builds alongside. CPU contention alone is mild; memory pressure
and builds are what turns it into stalls. Playing on a quiet machine is
the immediate remedy.

## In the game itself — to do

1. **Every song start stalls ≥ 250 ms** (Bevy clamps the delta there, so
   it may be longer), between "starting …" and the music: stage spawn,
   the microphone opening for the monitors, the telemetry store opening.
   It falls in the count-in and costs no notes, but it is a visible jerk.
   Seen in every run.
2. **A 20–25 ms frame every few seconds even on a quiet machine** —
   RESOLVED (2026-10-09): The hitch was caused by synchronous `info!`
   logging on the main render thread every ~5 seconds (`monitors: level ...`
   in `gameplay/monitors.rs` running on a 5.0 s timer, and `strip ...`
   in `gameplay/lightshow.rs` starting effects). Changed to `debug!`
   to prevent stdout/terminal lock delays during gameplay frames.
3. **Already being fixed in parallel:** the committed librarian built its
   work list on the main thread — 7 270 `song.json` reads from the
   external SSD in one frame — and kept fingerprinting and decoding in
   the background whenever no song was playing. The uncommitted change
   reads the list on a worker and starts new visits only in the idle
   main menu.

## How it was measured, and the traps

- Raise the window every 2.5 s; a covered window is paced differently.
- **`sample` stalls the process it samples**: in run 2 the only slow
  stretches were the two sampling windows. Do not read frame times from
  a run that was being sampled.
- A stress process started with `cmd &` in zsh and killed with
  `kill $LIST` does not die — zsh does not split the variable. Use
  `pkill -x yes` and check with `pgrep`.
